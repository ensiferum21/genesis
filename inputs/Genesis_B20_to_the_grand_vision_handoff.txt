1. Mission, finish lines and current position

The intended system discovers useful mathematical descriptions and procedures, verifies their meaning, uses them on new problems, and learns better ways to conduct further research. It should search over definitions, representations, algorithms and research procedures; maintain competing worlds with explicit assumptions; choose informative experiments; search for constructions and upper bounds together; and retain only capabilities whose correctness and utility survive the appropriate checks.

The central execution chain is:

Proposal → typed executable artifact → bounded execution → independent checking → immutable evidence → fresh prospective evaluation → retention or rejection.

1.1 Three finish lines

|Finish line                         |Required outcome                                                                                                                                                                                |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Bounded engineering release         |Declared modules integrate, enforce limits, preserve evidence, survive adversarial checks and reproduce on a supported independent host.                                                        |
|Validated mathematical grand vision |Useful checked discoveries, causal utility where claimed, a better research procedure, at least three distinct confirmed successor transitions, meaningful transfer and independent replication.|
|Extended mathematical-science vision|Separately validated model-training, software and empirical/instrument branches in addition to the mathematical system.                                                                         |

Engineering work can finish with EFFECT_NOT_SUPPORTED. That is an honest experimental outcome, but it does not pass a positive efficacy gate. Unlimited improvement, discovering every truth and superiority to every specialist solver are not finite acceptance criteria.

1.2 What B20 currently establishes

• Substantial evidence, sandbox, custody, formal-workspace and experiment infrastructure exists.
• B17–B20 contain bounded formal discovery, representation, world, optimization and research-learning implementations and DEV experiments.
• B20 implements five research policies, lemma-library successors, replayable macros, memory and curricula.
• Learned versus fixed is reported as one win and seven ties across eight DEV runs. The better-improver claim is explicitly unestablished.
• Three distinct evaluation statements appear in training development data, occupying four evaluation slots. The advertised global holdout property is false for those records.
• The learned policy’s only win uses 1.93 research CPU seconds against fixed’s 1.01, under a nominal one-second cap. Budget enforcement and accounting need repair.
• The 67 successful Lean replays out of 68 attempts cover fixed/learned on the first evaluation seed in each system, restricted to successful target proofs using library lemmas. They are not coverage of every reported score.
• The inherited B19 obstruction experiment preserves outcome summaries but not its complete finite-instance witness/LP-certificate evidence.
• R-04, GV0, independent custody, prospective efficacy and multigeneration acceptance remain open.

4. Protected invariants and execution boundaries

1. The candidate cannot write evaluator code, private panels, trust roots, signing keys, accepted releases or its resource authority.
2. Every attempt has a stable identity and a terminal or explicitly outstanding status. Failures, retries, timeouts, cancellations, interrupted jobs and human repairs remain charged and visible.
3. Freeze code, environment, solver, tools, memory, policy, curriculum, model configuration, task semantics and analysis before the relevant comparison.
4. A valid signature authenticates a statement; it does not make an unrelated, negative, malformed or unsupported statement useful evidence.
5. Exact theorem checking, empirical utility, causal attribution, novelty, replication and research-policy quality are separate claims.
6. Revocation follows dependencies into macros, memories, successor versions, reports and exports. Preserve historical decisions while recomputing effective status.
7. A candidate’s reported score is never the judge’s authoritative score. The judge recomputes from bounded execution and checker artifacts.
8. Missing proof, unsupported syntax and timeout must remain distinct from falsity. No countermodel found is not proof or a consistency certificate.
9. A construction supplies a lower bound. Optimality needs a matching independently valid upper bound for exactly the same instance and objective.
10. Model output, generated Python/Lean, solver output and retrieved documents are untrusted inputs. Only reviewed contracts cross the acceptance boundary.
11. Human help and shared operator control must be declared. Several keys or fresh agents under one operator do not establish institutional independence.
12. Protect the scientific endpoint: do not replace “fresh-task utility after cost” with number of lemmas, shorter text, test count, generation count or architectural breadth.

7. Repair A: globally disjoint research/evaluation material

Finding: B20-REVIEW-01. Relevant files: scripts/b20_learning.py, src/genesis/learning/equational.py, learning/memory.py, kernel/leakscan.py, exposure.py, and evidence/learning_b20/setup.json.

7.1 Reproduce before editing

Compare every evaluation statement for seeds 201–204 against the union of development statements for training seeds 101–103, separately by system. B20 contains one overlap at Huntington 201, one at 203, and two at 204, all sourced from training seed 103. There are three unique overlapping statements. Save the exact statements, source locations and comparison program under a new review-repair audit directory.

The source samples each seed’s evaluation targets with exclude=pool + dev for that seed only. Training evaluates on development targets and derives usefulness labels/features from them. Fresh seeds therefore do not imply globally fresh content.

7.2 Proposed implementation

Add one reusable partition builder rather than another experiment-specific filter. A suggested location is src/genesis/learning/partitions.py; first check whether an existing exposure service can supply the behavior directly.

1. Define a MaterialIdentity from domain semantics, signature, assumptions and canonical statement. Record both exact text hash and structural identity; distinguish incompatible theories even if text matches.
2. Generate all research-visible material first: training pools, training development targets, accessible proofs, cached knowledge and starting memory. Freeze its manifest and dependency closure.
3. Generate evaluation material against that global exposure set. Within a study, cluster related tasks and partition clusters where appropriate. Also screen later research seeds against reserved evaluation material through the custodian’s controlled interface.
4. Check exact equality, variable renaming, syntax normalization and declared substitution-instance relations. Equational commutativity or theory-dependent equivalence requires explicit normalization semantics; do not claim universal semantic deduplication.
5. Include imports, macro expansions and generated curriculum outputs. Screening only task ingestion leaves capability-insertion paths exposed.
6. On an ambiguous near-duplicate, quarantine it with a reason. On insufficient unique tasks, report INSUFFICIENT_DISTINCT_MATERIAL; do not weaken the policy to fill a requested sample count.
7. Freeze partition algorithm/version, RNG derivation, cluster map, exposure root, rejection counts and accepted identities before training. Any change creates a new study identity.

7.3 Private versus DEV operation

The builder may inspect all DEV material and learn how the partitioner works. Production confirmation material must be generated/reserved privately. Do not expose sealed statements or unsalted hashes of a small enumerable space. Use a custodian-held salt/commitment and controlled aggregate screening responses. Rate-limit screening and record queries so it cannot be used to reconstruct the hidden pool.

Returning similarity scores, nearest sealed statements or exact match explanations to the research worker can leak private tasks. Detailed adjudication belongs in private evidence, with an appropriately coarse public result.

7.4 Acceptance tests

• The original four overlapping slots are detected without any prover installed.
• Cross-seed exact, alpha-renamed and declared instance collisions are rejected.
• Every route that inserts a lemma/macro/curriculum item is screened, including procedural additions.
• No private task text appears in worker files, logs, exception messages or ordinary screening responses.
• Insufficient diversity fails explicitly; deterministic reconstruction returns the same public DEV manifest.
• Unrelated valid tasks remain admissible, avoiding an always-reject implementation.
• Newly contaminated or exposed panels are consumed/retired for the affected claim; old results remain labelled contaminated.

Exit: global partition audit passes, a fresh study is frozen, and the evidence manifest records residual screening limits. A repair does not sanitize B20’s old comparison.

8. Repair B: enforce and account for research budgets

Finding: B20-REVIEW-02. Relevant files: learning/research.py::run, learning/macros.py::expand, verification/eprover.py, resources.py, sandbox.py, launcher.py and all B20 research scripts.

8.1 Root cause and reproduction

Policy reset happens before the CPU timer. The loop checks the cap before action selection, then passes max(1, ceil(remaining)) through a per-attempt limit. It accepts successful results after the campaign cap. The recorded Huntington seed 204 comparison is fixed 1.01 s, 24/25; learned 1.93 s, 25/25, under a nominal 1 s cap. Heuristic and bandit also reach 25/25 around 1 s. Preserve these facts without asserting which timing difference caused which proof.

8.2 Resource model

Reuse the existing resource authority and launcher instead of trusting candidate timers. Introduce a strict study budget record with integer CPU nanoseconds, wall nanoseconds, memory bytes, attempt/call caps and concurrency. Track research, training, verification and downstream evaluation separately, and report their full total.

Choose one declared competitor budget policy before evaluation:

• Full-cost: training/setup/induction and use are charged to the same finite campaign.
• Amortized: immutable training cost is spread across a preregistered number of uses, with full cost still reported.

Never choose the amortization denominator after seeing performance. Equal caps need not force an early-finishing arm to burn unused compute. What is required is consistent access, charging, ceilings and acceptance semantics.

8.3 Scheduler contract

Start accounting before policy preparation. Measure all descendants of the research worker, not only one direct prover. Use aggregate process/cgroup accounting where available; a Python process_time clock alone is insufficient for subprocess work. Avoid double-counting process and child totals.

Reserve resources before starting an attempt. If the prover only supports whole-second CPU limits, use an admissible quantum with a declared overhead reserve, or supervise the entire process group under an outer hard budget. Do not round a fractional remainder up into extra credited work. If there is no admissible quantum, record INSUFFICIENT_REMAINING_BUDGET and stop.

Conceptual sequence, not an existing API:

open campaign resource account
charge preparation and policy initialization
while account can reserve an admissible action:
    choose action under that same account
    reserve remaining allowed execution and checking resources
    execute in isolated worker with outer CPU and wall enforcement
    store raw output, usage, termination reason and resource-authority receipt
    check artifact under the registered charging policy
    accept it only if the declared budget/late-result rule permits it
    settle reservation; retain interrupted or rejected attempts
freeze successor from eligible checked artifacts

A hard kill has finite scheduling granularity. Specify a measured enforcement tolerance for diagnostics; do not turn it into an unrestricted source of useful extra work. If the authority cannot establish that a proof was completed within its admissible budget, classify the attempt according to the frozen rule, ordinarily not crediting it. Use substantially longer research caps than the granularity when measuring small effects.

LP solving, exact deciders, feature construction, retrieval, macro induction, curriculum creation and policy updates also consume resources. Route potentially long native-library calls through bounded workers; a timeout on the later SAT call does not bound an earlier LP call.

8.4 Required records and tests

Record requested/reserved/consumed CPU and wall budgets, preparation cost, prover cost, checker cost, peak memory, attempt ID, parent account, launch/finish status, result eligibility and policy identity. Keep raw output even when it cannot earn score.

Tests must cover: no launch with insufficient quantum; expensive policy reset/selection; slow exact decider; subprocess grandchildren; timeout/kill; successful output after deadline; interrupted reservations; restart without free retries; macro expansion and curriculum using the same authority; and baseline/candidate receiving the same charging rules. Include at least one real slow subprocess test in addition to deterministic fake-clock tests.

Exit: bounded stress runs stay within declared authority limits, late work cannot improve credited successors, all pathways are charged, and a fresh policy comparison uses the corrected mechanism.

10. Repair D: optimization certificate custody

Finding: B20-REVIEW-04. Relevant files: discovery/obstruction.py::certify, scripts/b19_discovery.py, verification/sat.py::certify_upper, verification/certificates.py, kernel/evidence.py, worldstep.py.

10.1 Scope of the finding

All 12 recorded B19 joint upper bounds use LP_DUAL_EXACT; 11 close the gap. The exported experiment rows retain bounds and verdict summaries but omit the final witness and exact LP certificate. The source also discards returned CNF/DRAT bytes in its SAT fallback. That fallback did not supply the upper certificates of these 12 recorded instances; do not describe them as failed DRAT checks.

10.2 Certificate bundle contract

For every proposed bound retain:

• Canonical task bytes, domain contract, allowed host edges and objective identity.
• Lower witness and independent feasibility/value check.
• Upper method discriminator: host-edge count, exact LP dual, SAT/DRAT, checked theorem or another explicitly supported method.
• Exact rational weights, constraints, objective and rounding convention for an LP certificate. Floating solver output is only a proposal until exact feasibility checks pass.
• Original CNF, encoding version and DRAT bytes for SAT certificates, with hashes and checker identity/output.
• Theorem statement, instantiation, assumptions, proof and checked conversion from theorem to numeric bound for theorem-based certificates.
• Attempt and cost records, toolchain identities and full dependency closure.

Do not alter the supported mathematical inequalities while fixing persistence. The replay path validates the stored lower witness and upper justification independently, verifies lower <= upper, and derives optimality only from equality of valid bounds on the same task.

10.3 Integration and tests

Return immutable evidence references from the obstruction loop; store them before result publication. Export those references and objects through the existing bundle system. Reuse existing bound-state and certificate schemas where their semantics fit; version extensions explicitly.

Test LP replay without optimization installed, SAT replay with only the checker available, changed rational coefficient, changed task, swapped witness, missing proof, corrupted CNF, unsupported method and revoked checker. Check that an incomplete bundle cannot retain OPTIMALITY_CHECKED through reports or generic promotion.

Recomputing an LP or rerunning SAT may produce a new valid result, but it is a new execution. It does not reconstruct the original missing evidence retroactively. Keep the old B19 observation and append a new custody-complete experiment.

Exit: all newly claimed bounds replay from their artifacts alone, and the previously disconnected optimization path obeys the same trust and revocation rules as the older world loop.

14. Experimental design and decision rules

Workstreams: W05–W07, E0–E6; B16-OPEN-2/B19-OPEN-4.

14.1 Study manifest

Freeze the question, target population, primary endpoint, practical effect threshold, baseline selection, randomization, allowed information, budgets, sample/cluster counts, stopping rule, exclusions, invalid-run policy, multiplicity family, analysis code and required replay coverage. Sign/anchor its identity before confirmation.

The B16 headroom model and B19 centered-headroom alternative need independent statistical review. Do not choose whichever power model makes a study affordable after observing confirmation results. Check via DEV simulations how ceiling rates, ties, family dependence, cost variance and expected effect change power. A prior suggestion of 700 theorems is not a universal requirement or guarantee.

14.2 Units of evidence

• E1: a frozen solver’s performance on fresh tasks; task-family clusters may define independence.
• E2: controlled concept/theory interventions; the contrast must isolate the proposed mechanism.
• E3: independent research runs produce successors, which are scored on untouched downstream tasks. More targets within one run improve measurement of that run, not the number of independent policy-learning runs.
• E4: ordered successor transitions plus a preregistered joint analysis. Three positive-looking point estimates alone do not prove compounding.
• E5: source-frozen capabilities evaluated after a declared target adaptation budget.
• E6: original artifact replay and a separately governed fresh efficacy experiment.

Use paired task panels across comparison arms where valid; isolate arm memory and feedback. Randomize or counterbalance execution order, record host load, and declare retries. Repeat DEV measurements to estimate runtime variability before sizing confirmation. A rerun triggered by an unfavorable result must not replace the original observation.

14.3 Metrics and cost

Choose one primary utility measure appropriate to the task, such as checked solved fraction at fixed resource limits or certified gap reduction. Report computation, model calls, memory, storage, verification, training/induction and human intervention costs separately and together. A score averaged across unrelated scales needs an explicit weighting contract.

Compare learned research against fixed, random, tuned heuristic and an appropriate bandit/search baseline. For solver claims, include strong conventional methods applicable to the chosen family. Do not call a per-task oracle choice a deployable baseline; report it as a diagnostic ceiling unless a real selector earns its selection cost.

Freeze selection before CONFIRM. Do not keep retrying candidates on the same panel. Preserve the current conservative treatment of outstanding family registrations; any change to multiplicity or sequential analysis needs review and executable tests.

14.4 Decision semantics

SUPPORTED, NOT_SUPPORTED, INCONCLUSIVE and INVALID are distinct. An unsuccessful but valid run contributes evidence. A broken harness may invalidate a comparison and require a fresh study after repair. Define practically useful gain and unacceptable regression before seeing confirmation.

If the study has no power or little headroom, report that at readiness. Do not launch a nominal sealed experiment merely to accumulate a checkpoint. Each accepted claim must be recomputable from signed raw-evidence commitments and the registered analysis.

15. Formal research, historical concept recovery and representations

Workstreams: W08–W10; B17-OPEN-1/2 and B18-OPEN-1/2.

15.1 Formal workspace and open-ended proof search

B17’s formal workspace, binding and proof acceptance are valuable foundations. Its proof strategies are largely builder-written recipes. Extend the search layer with typed tactics/program synthesis or a configured proposal model, while keeping the trusted checker unchanged. Accessible model calls and paid budgets must actually exist before using them; the current configuration permits no paid API expenditure.

Preserve every proposed statement, tactic, failed elaboration, timeout and dependency. A search process can propose arbitrary candidate text only inside the declared syntax and sandbox limits. Reject sorry, undeclared axioms, environment changes and proof/statement substitution through the existing acceptance path.

Use the pinned workspace build/audit tooling. Record full source and compiled artifact identities. Independent replay of theorem packages, not merely unit tests of helper functions, is needed to accept formal capability.

15.2 Historical concept recovery

B18’s recovery experiment uses a generated history library and recovers a known Antitone-like abstraction. Keep it as a controlled demonstration. To implement the historical-library requirement:

1. Select and freeze a dated Mathlib snapshot with provenance and dependency closure.
2. Extract definitions, theorem statements, proof structure and import graph available at that cutoff.
3. Construct separate historical DEV tasks and future evaluation families; remove future imports, names, comments and cached artifacts from the research view.
4. Compute repeated typed patterns using anti-unification and dependency/proof-cost statistics.
5. Generate conservative definitions and candidate lemmas; reify in Lean and check their exact assumptions.
6. Compile successful concepts into reusable methods and measure fresh-family utility.
7. Compare against ordinary retrieval, caching, extra search effort and a supplied equivalent abstraction.

A model trained after the historical cutoff may know future concepts. Historical restriction of repository files alone cannot certify model ignorance. Record model provenance, use deterministic structural baselines, and qualify the historical novelty claim accordingly.

Suggested new module: formal_research/history.py or a small separate ingestion package. Avoid a large new indexing service until local manifests and SQLite are demonstrably insufficient.

15.3 Richer representation invention

The current grammar primarily expresses vertex-class decoders. Extend one representation family at a time: exact derived predicates, partitions/quotients, decompositions, relational encodings and reduction maps. Each proposal must declare source/target types, encoder, decoder, assumptions, retained/lost information, objective relation and computation limits.

For quotienting, prove the conditions that make transport valid; an arbitrary partition is not automatically an equivalence-preserving quotient. For relaxations, distinguish an upper bound from an exact equivalence. For symmetry, prove objective and feasibility preservation before pruning.

Generate candidates compositionally under bounded MRDL/IR sizes. Reuse declared limits and version any justified expansion; do not silently remove bounds to fit a result. Compare a learned representation with the fixed menu, conventional features and matched additional search.

Acceptance: at least one machine-generated representation outside the hard-coded menu has checked semantics, a valid decoder/transport path and prospective utility after construction cost. A renamed existing feature meets none of those requirements by itself.

19. Learned research policies, macros, memory and curricula

Workstreams: W18–W20; all four B20-OPEN items. Implement repairs in Sections 7–9 first.

19.1 Diagnose before increasing model complexity

The current learned policy’s predictions are nearly uninformative and most main-run tasks have little headroom. Start with DEV measurements that distinguish potential causes: target difficulty, absence of useful library reuse, weak features, labels rewarding irrelevant lemmas, unstable timing, insufficient training diversity and the advantage of simple fixed ordering.

Freeze separate development studies for each diagnosis. Use learning curves, cost curves, calibration plots and per-family failure summaries. A valid negative result is useful. Do not spend a sealed panel selecting the model, feature set or target family.

19.2 Research-policy learning

Retain fixed, random, heuristic and bandit baselines. Extend the learned action space gradually: conjecture choice, premise retrieval, experiment allocation, stopping and capability retirement. Avoid simultaneous changes that prevent attribution.

Train on features available before the action, such as typed structure, prior DEV failure categories, library overlap and previously measured costs. Do not include future success, hidden task identity or evaluator-only difficulty. Label usefulness from checked outcomes and, when feasible, interventions that distinguish an actually useful lemma from one merely present in a successful library.

Cross-fit or hold out whole research families when tuning; freeze weights and preprocessing before the E3 study. Version training data, feature extractor, optimizer/settings, random seeds and training cost. A larger model is a research choice that must earn its cost, not a completion requirement.

Done when: independent research runs show that the frozen learner produces better checked successors than strong fixed alternatives at the declared outer cost, on unused downstream tasks. More lemmas or higher training accuracy is insufficient.

19.3 Macros and generative compression

Current macros are ordered schemas derived from used-lemma closure. Faithful replay and shorter text are implemented, but fresh-task advantage is unestablished and strict transfer can fail at the first step.

Extend parameterization by typed anti-unification across multiple successful DEV trajectories. Specify semantic preconditions beyond operation arity when the macro requires algebraic laws. Preserve source proof dependencies; transfer may search for new proofs under target axioms but may not assume source lemmas automatically.

Separate a macro’s reference expansion from optional search for alternative dependency paths. Strict and non-strict semantics must be explicit and versioned. Non-strict execution earns only the lemmas it newly proves/checks; it does not inherit skipped proof status.

Compare parameterized macro, recorded replay, conventional templates, plain search and cached lemmas. Charge induction, failed induction, screening, replay and maintenance. Test whether a macro produces useful new trajectories or merely replays its training trace. Revoke a source dependency and verify invalidation through every dependent version.

19.4 Memory and curriculum

Give memory entries globally unique content identities with theory, assumptions, provenance, proof status, exposure class, applicability, retrieval/maintenance cost and revocation dependencies. Local L0-style names cannot identify an entry across runs.

Compare no memory, exact caching, conventional retrieval, replay-all and learned retrieval/retirement. Track interference: B20 already shows an irrelevant library can harm E. Evaluate retention on a declared legacy panel without tuning repeatedly on it.

Generate curricula only from allowed DEV failures. Validate each synthetic task’s semantics, provenance, difficulty and solvability status. Synthetic tasks are training material, not fresh evaluation. Charge task generation and solvability checking as well as research on the generated tasks.

Define the boundary between prohibited answer leakage and permitted general knowledge in advance. Blanket rejection of every broad lemma that can instantiate to a goal can remove the very abstraction benefit being tested. Use a strict no-near-answer condition where that is the scientific question, while separately allowing declared public/reference theory in every arm; report direct-instance solving separately when appropriate. The policy cannot be relaxed after observing gains.

Done when: memory/curriculum beats conventional reuse at total cost on a meaningful fresh distribution, with controlled exposure and no unacceptable forgetting. Otherwise retain useful infrastructure and record that the proposed learning mechanism remains unestablished.

20.2 Proposed transition lifecycle

The lifecycle below is conceptual. Map it onto the existing signed phase machine; do not silently replace its phases.

|State                        |Required record                                                         |Allowed next action                                              |
|-----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
|Parent frozen                |Complete parent manifest and trust/exposure state                       |Register a research plan                                         |

E3 compares research policies: each starts from the same declared initial solver/memory/information and receives an equal outer budget, produces a successor, then that successor receives fresh downstream evaluation. Every transition needs a matching parent/child and hypothesis identity. • Parent exists and was frozen before this research/evaluation sequence.
• Child’s declared parent and changed components match the actual manifests.
• Every dependency is present, eligible, unrevoked and inside permitted information.
• Branch selection follows the predeclared rule and counts all attempts.

• The same transition cannot be presented under three wrapper IDs as three generations.

A valid request includes both research-level comparisons and downstream evaluation structure. Do not treat several thousand target rows from one research run as several thousand independent improver trials. The analysis contract must reflect run-level clustering.

20.6 Recovery, rollback and retention

Reserve an attempt in the durable store before launching it. Use idempotent stable attempt IDs and transactional state changes. A crash after launch but before commit creates an interrupted charged attempt, not a free retry. A frozen successor never changes on resume.

Keep the previous accepted version immutable. Rollback changes the active pointer and records why; it does not erase the failed child. If a retained capability is revoked, traverse dependent versions and recompute effective claims. Resume only from a state whose required dependencies remain valid.

Measure legacy retention and negative transfer under a frozen protocol. Repeated evaluation on the same public retention tasks can itself become training; maintain protected retention confirmation material where the claim requires it.

20.7 Acceptance tests

Required positive case: a small deterministic DEV fixture runs through at least three distinct transitions, exports all artifacts, survives crash/restart and replays. Label fixture outcomes as fixtures. Scientific acceptance additionally requires three qualifying prospective transitions and a valid joint analysis; the DEV fixture cannot satisfy GV5.

21. B21: primitive invention and reasoning reliability

Workstreams: W22/W23. Implement within existing IR/MRDL and trust boundaries.

21.1 Primitive proposal contract

Analyze repeated DEV traces for expensive or missing operations. Propose a typed primitive with input/output types, preconditions, semantics, deterministic reference expansion where possible, effects, failure modes, resource bounds, implementation digest and a hypothesis about downstream benefit.

Start with derived primitives that expand into already accepted operations. These can shorten search descriptions without expanding the trusted computing base. Compare them with the equivalent macro and reference expansion; naming a macro “primitive” is not a capability gain.

An optimized native implementation needs conformance testing and a correctness/refinement argument appropriate to its effect. Tests can catch bugs but are not a universal semantic proof. Proof-bearing results still pass the independent checker.

A genuinely new trusted checker or semantic foundation requires an external trust decision. The primitive proposer cannot register itself as its own authoritative verifier. Keep an unapproved primitive experimental and prevent it from licensing accepted claims.

21.2 IR evolution

Version primitive contracts and include their digest in SystemVersion.cognitive_kernel. Persist reference expansions, old interpreter/version identities and migration adapters. Preserve the ability to replay historical research programs with their original semantics.

Test deterministic serialization, type errors, malformed parameters, excessive depth/size, integer overflow limits, forbidden effects, recursion/cycles, nondeterministic implementations and resource exhaustion. A new opcode must not gain authority to access network/secrets/evaluator files or alter its budget.

Accept a proposed primitive as useful only after a fresh comparison against the incumbent language, its equivalent macro and conventional alternatives including induction/verification cost.

21.3 Fault-tolerant reasoning

Use heterogeneous evidence routes when available: dual graph predicates, exact arithmetic, formal proof replay, independent finite-model evaluation and reference implementations. Diversity is useful only when the routes have meaningfully different failure modes. Several agents sharing the same model and prompt do not create independent mathematical evidence.

On disagreement, quarantine the artifact, preserve both inputs/results and classify possible causes: different assumptions, parser translation, unsupported proof rule, numeric approximation, resource failure or checker defect. Do not resolve mathematical disagreements by majority vote.

21.4 Expert interaction

Create a structured expert question from a specific unresolved decision, with minimal relevant artifacts and explicit alternatives. Record question, answer, assumptions, respondent/controller, timing, resulting changes and cost. Expert testimony on intent/priority is different from a checked mathematical proof.

Do not silently use expert answers to tune a confirmed candidate. Any intervention that affects a frozen system produces a new version and may require a new study. The agent should prepare the review package first; contacting a person or sending a message requires the user’s appropriate authorization.

Acceptance: injected translation/checker disagreements fail closed, useful independent routes detect real faults, and any expert-assisted benefit is evaluated within the declared assistance budget and autonomy scope.

22. B22: transfer and independent replication

Workstream: W24; GV6/GV7.

22.1 Transfer object and target choice

C4-free to triangle-free is useful near-domain transfer; it is insufficient alone for the broader claim. 22.2 Two replication jobs

Job A: artifact reproduction. Another operator verifies exact source/tool identities and replays the original proofs/certificates, using their own trusted installation. It may legitimately reproduce the original finite result without rerunning discovery.

Job B: fresh efficacy replication. The independent operator runs a new registered evaluation on new protected material, preserving baselines and cost rules. This tests persistence of the effect rather than only integrity of the old artifact.

The same-host clean-room report provides neither separate operational control nor fresh efficacy by itself. Record each job and its limitations separately.

Deliver source/archive hash, build inputs, immutable manifests, replay artifacts, private/public evidence separation, analysis code, all outcomes, exact accepted claims, invalidated/unsupported claims, custody/operator declarations and the external decision. Novelty requires its own priority review; transfer success is not proof of a new mathematical domain.

Compare fine-tuning against retrieval, added tool libraries and equal-compute search. Measure forgetting, calibration and robustness. A hosted model’s identifier may not guarantee immutable weights; record provider uncertainty.

Do not pretend an API prompt update is a neural training result. Mechanistic claims require interventions on the alleged mechanism, not merely feature visualizations.

23.2 W26 — Software research

Add an isolated repository-domain adapter with exact commits, dependency locks, problem statements, test contracts, hidden independent tests and patch artifacts. Reproduce a bug before fixing it; prevent the candidate from modifying hidden evaluators or redefining success. Bound subprocess/network access and preserve all edits and attempts.

For performance algorithms, use stable benchmarking conditions, correctness tests and total development cost. Evaluate across unseen repositories/projects with project-level dependence accounted for. Distinguish a useful patch, an improved coding agent and a better coding-agent improver.

23.3 W27 — Numerical/empirical/instrument science

Separate exact or interval-certified numerical claims from empirical observations. Define units, observation model, instrument calibration, uncertainty, intervention authority and identifiability. Simulation observations stay labelled simulation; they do not certify physical predictions.

Begin with an adapter that records proposed experiment, admissible controls, simulator/real instrument identity, raw measurements, transformations, uncertainty and independent replication. Real actions require the actual authorized operator and domain controls. Do not fabricate measurements when hardware/access is absent.

Accept an empirical discovery only after new real observations support its prediction under the registered design. “No available measurement distinguishes these hypotheses” is a valid unresolved result.

23.4 W28 — Economics and unattended operation

Maintain campaign-level CPU/wall/model-call/storage/human-cost accounts; measure queueing, failed work, verification bottlenecks and memory-maintenance cost. Keep one persistent writer unless measured throughput requires a reviewed architecture change.

Implement bounded scheduling, backpressure, stop reasons, atomic checkpoints, safe resume, artifact retention and recovery drills. Do not garbage-collect objects reachable from accepted, audited or pending claims. Failed attempts may be compacted only under an explicit retention policy preserving scientific accounting and required evidence.

Set campaign ceilings and a stop policy before launch. Run an unattended endurance test of declared duration/workload; inject crashes, missing tools, full disks, corrupted cache objects and unavailable external services. Compare final accounts with durable attempts and launcher records. No duplicate free work, forgotten reservations or silent loss of negative outcomes is acceptable.

A plateau should trigger the registered stop/redesign rule, not endless compute justified by the phrase “improve forever.” The system may discover additional gaps indefinitely, but finite budgets and diminishing returns remain operational facts.

25.4 Statistical and scientific tests are different
