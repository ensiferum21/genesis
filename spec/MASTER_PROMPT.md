# Gap Engine → Genesis: master upgrade prompt

Use this prompt with **Gap Engine — Concepts and Implementation Specification.pdf** attached. Attach earlier Genesis handovers and the current repository if available. The Genesis requirements below are self-contained; inaccessible historical material must be marked unavailable.

---

You are the lead research architect and implementation engineer upgrading my existing Gap Engine toward the Genesis vision.

Read the attached Gap Engine specification completely before proposing changes. Produce one coherent, implementation-ready specification and migration plan. If a working repository is also supplied, inspect the actual implementation and carry out the authorized, reversible implementation work in staged checkpoints. If no repository is supplied, deliver the complete specification and handoff without claiming that code has been implemented or tested.

## 1. Objective and meaning of Genesis

Genesis means a bounded, externally evaluated research system that can:

- Find failures and blind spots in its knowledge and research methods.
- Invent useful concepts, representations, hypotheses, experiments, measurements and tools.
- Turn checked discoveries into reusable capabilities and transfer or recombine them.
- Improve problem selection, curricula, research policies, specialist organization and permitted internal research architectures.
- Test whether those changes improve both problem solving and the production of further improvements.

Optimize verified progress on externally specified goals and fresh tasks under explicit total resource budgets. Treat long-term research acceleration as an experimental hypothesis. Adding modules, agents, generated code, theories or impressive terminology does not establish progress.

Make Gap Engine the research core of this larger system. Preserve and extend working mechanisms. Do not restart with a renamed architecture or assume every Genesis ambition is already implementable or scientifically solved.

## 2. Start with a source-grounded comparison

Create a requirements map covering every requirement of the original PDF and every Genesis requirement in this prompt. For each, identify the existing section, actual implementation if inspectable, required change, dependencies, evidence and acceptance test.

Distinguish PRESENT, PARTIAL, MISSING, CONFLICTING and UNVERIFIED requirements. For repository integration, preserve the inventory statuses INSPECTED_REUSABLE, INSPECTED_NEEDS_REPAIR, MISSING_FROM_REACHABLE_CORPUS and UNVERIFIED. Inspect functions and call paths; filenames and test counts are insufficient evidence of capability.

Recognize features already present, including research islands, model populations, instrument requests, concept packages, transfer, consolidation and descendant evaluation. Extend their missing mechanisms instead of duplicating them.

Use available Genesis/Foundry/Prime, Ladder, Keystone, Ghost Census, TCSR and Theory Engine sources as historical inputs. Do not invent missing contents, reconstruct alleged experimental results from names, or treat prior assistant claims as verified evidence. Preserve claim-ladder meanings through an explicit compatibility map.

## 3. Preserve the evidence and authority boundaries

Keep the original invariants and strengthen ambiguous implementations. Any correction to an inconsistent requirement needs an explicit rationale, compatibility impact and replacement test.

The trusted constitution, acceptance criteria, evaluation tasks, checkers, budgets, credentials, ledger and deployment authority remain outside the autonomous research mutation loop. They are fixed for a campaign. The system may submit a proposed future change for external review; it cannot grant itself authority or certify that change.

Separate proposer, executor, checker and promotion responsibilities through enforced permissions. Different agent names alone do not create independence. Generated code receives restricted interfaces and isolated workspaces; it cannot overwrite protected tasks, evidence, evaluators, accepted releases or the active launcher.

Preserve full attempt accounting, immutable artifacts, externally anchored evidence, brokered paid calls, preregistration, contamination controls and disjoint DEV/SELECT/CONFIRM/FUTURE partitions. Confirmation results cannot select or tune another candidate for the same claim. Both comparison arms receive the task information necessary to execute the test; hidden answers, private evaluation machinery and future outcomes remain protected.

Retain the separation between ENGINEERING_COMPLETE, EXPERIMENT_COMPLETE, CLAIM_SUPPORTED and EXTERNALLY_REPLICATED. Mocks, fixtures and simulations must carry their scope; they cannot establish live or real-world success. Missing dependencies return an explicit unsupported or blocked state, never fabricated metrics.

## 4. Extend the research machinery in concrete ways

### A. Research agenda and problem formulation

Add a research-agenda mechanism that proposes questions and curricula from external goals, recurring failures, uncertainty and plausible downstream usefulness. Reserve bounded exploration for unfamiliar problem families.

Permit reformulation, decomposition, changed coordinates and intermediate objectives. Every transformation must record its relationship to the original task: equivalence, implication, relaxation, strengthening or conjectural analogy, with the appropriate check. Preserve original statements and success criteria. Solving an easier surrogate is a separate result.

### B. Competing explanations and representation invention

Extend the existing model population to maintain alternative causal structures, mechanisms, predictive states and representations. Design affordable interventions that distinguish their predictions. Track observational equivalence, uncertainty and assumptions.

Represent the starting grammar, available constructors and permitted grammar extensions explicitly. A new primitive needs executable semantics, a type or interface, examples, counterexamples and novel testable consequences. Evaluate extensions against fixed-language and enumeration baselines at matched total cost. Describe escape from a grammar relative to that declared grammar; do not claim escape from all possible languages.

### C. Measurement and instrument development

Turn MeasurementSpec requests into a complete lifecycle: propose, predict discriminating outcomes, estimate cost, authorize access, implement in a sandbox or simulator, calibrate, check independent observations and assess fresh-task benefit.

Start with software probes and simulated sensors. Model predictions must never become observations. Actual laboratory access, hardware operations, purchases and new external permissions require the corresponding existing authorization or a concrete request for it. Unavailable physical capabilities produce a reviewable request, not a fictional experiment.

Compare new sensing against cheaper interventions, longer observation and existing instruments. Distinguish identifying a world from improving predictions within a restricted model family.

### D. Reusable capabilities and recombination

Extend concept packages into typed capability packages where useful. Include executable interfaces, applicability conditions, assumptions, dependencies, provenance, resource requirements, examples, known failure cases, maintenance cost and rollback information.

Support discovery, composition, transfer, retirement and revalidation. Composition must check assumptions and interface compatibility; it does not inherit correctness or permissions automatically. Imported packages must earn value in the receiving domain. Track negative transfer and library growth costs.

Distinguish representation equivalence, proof reuse, algorithm reuse and empirical transfer. Cross-domain analogy proposes a candidate; each domain supplies its own verification.

### E. Temporary specialists and independent lineages

Extend research islands with budgeted specialist lifecycles: instantiate, assign scope, evaluate, retain, merge or retire. Each specialist has a role contract, tool permissions, budget, state and output schema.

A specialist can initially be a prompted instance of an existing model or a deterministic solver. Do not require training new models to implement this mechanism.

Preserve useful diversity through measured differences in hypotheses, strategies and outcomes. Compare the ecology with a single strong agent and a fixed portfolio using identical total resources. More agents alone is not improvement. Count coordination, failed branches and integration costs.

### F. Permitted research-architecture evolution

Extend policy editing into bounded architecture proposals. Start with a versioned, typed graph of research components: proposer routing, workflow decomposition, memory/retrieval organization, specialist topology, tool composition and scheduling.

Specify an allowlist of legal mutations and validate interfaces, capabilities, resource bounds and state migrations. Build successors in isolated workspaces, test them as challengers and promote atomically through the external evaluator. Preserve the parent, migration provenance and rollback path.

Search may propose new implementations behind approved interfaces. It may not mutate the trusted runtime, grant permissions, redefine correctness or edit its own evaluation data. Evaluate architecture changes against ordinary prompt tuning, policy optimization and simpler workflow changes to identify the source of any gain.

### G. Learned research strategy and better-improver tests

Learn which experiments, representations, tools and research strategies produce checked progress for which task families. Include unsuccessful, cancelled and invalid attempts in the evidence and accounting.

Separate better solving, better research allocation and better generation of future improvements. For the better-improver claim, start replicated improvement runs from parent and child under equal outer-loop budgets, then evaluate the resulting successors on untouched tasks. Compare distributions across preregistered repetitions and generations, including failures, rather than selecting one champion lineage.

Forecasts are evidence about calibration. Keep forecast quality distinguishable from capability improvement; document how any forecast-based gate affects interpretation and avoid conflating a poor forecast with proof of no capability gain.

### H. Optional model learning

Keep fine-tuning or distillation a separately gated extension requiring accessible weights, suitable data permissions and explicit compute budgets. Include training, data generation and evaluation costs, retention tests and fresh transfer tasks.

Never imply access to hosted-model weights. If training is unavailable, mark that extension UNSUPPORTED and keep the other research mechanisms executable.

## 5. Repair tensions in the current specification

Address these explicitly before adding complexity:

1. **Delayed usefulness.** The direct-value-first rule for descendant evaluation can exclude concepts whose only benefit appears through later discoveries. Add a small preregistered exploration allocation for scientifically admissible candidates with uncertain or negative immediate utility. Define selection without future-task access, candidate and spend caps, horizons and termination criteria. Speculative candidates remain quarantined; this path does not waive evidence requirements or guarantee retention.
2. **Mixed diagnoses.** The four gap categories need not be mutually exclusive. Preserve multiple hypotheses where justified, permit revisions and avoid forcing every failure through a single definitive label.
3. **Different strengths of certificate.** Distinguish formal witnesses, statistical evidence and budget-scoped empirical failures. A cost cliff across tested solvers is not a universal lower bound; a noisy collision is not an unconditional proof; bounded model search failure is not entailment or impossibility. Define checker contracts and assumptions accordingly.
4. **Validation versus value.** Test the productivity meter using the PDF's required controls, document any revisions and separate retrospective human reuse from prospective benefit. Do not claim a finite horizon measures ultimate scientific importance.
5. **Search overhead.** Measure discovery, diagnostics, coordination, checking, integration, training where applicable and maintenance. Report evaluation cost separately as well as total campaign cost, with an explicit accounting convention that avoids double counting. Every added mechanism must justify its cost.
6. **Logical soundness.** Preserve Ghost Census semantics: an independently checked countermodel refutes entailment from the visible theory; no ghost found is not a proof; conservative definitions do not remove genuine countermodels. Prove additional lemmas from the permitted stronger theory through a controlled checker without leaking the oracle theory. Inconsistent contexts never count as useful mathematical success.

## 6. Require an executable contract for every extension

For each new or changed capability, supply:

- Purpose, scope, assumptions, non-goals and unresolved research questions.
- Inputs, outputs, strict schemas and state transitions.
- An initial implementable algorithm, pseudocode and concrete worked example.
- Interfaces, permissions, persisted state, crash recovery and resource limits.
- Dependencies, baseline, isolated ablation and end-to-end comparison.
- Failure modes, adversarial tests and numerical or explicitly configurable acceptance criteria.
- Evidence needed for each permitted claim and a stop, rollback or retirement rule.

Clearly label research hypotheses versus engineering decisions. Verify any cited external research using primary sources; provide links and explain exactly what it supports. Do not present a paper as evidence for the whole Genesis vision or claim novelty without a scoped search.

## 7. Validation and implementation order

Preserve the original experiment and claim identifiers. Give additions distinct identifiers and publish the compatibility map; build batches are not scientific claim levels.

Use a staged route:

1. Inventory, protected evidence kernel, authentic checkers and direct-solver baselines.
2. Existing Ghost Census Experiment 0, the meter-validation pilot and bounded simulated-world experiments, with their prerequisites satisfied.
3. Concept/capability packages, reuse and delayed-value experiments.
4. Research-agenda changes, reformulation and instrument-development experiments.
5. Specialist populations, transfer and architecture mutations, each with isolated ablations.
6. Replicated better-improver tests and independent external replication.
7. Optional model training and externally authorized physical experimentation.

Do not make all domains a prerequisite for the first useful result. Use formal mathematics and sealed simulated worlds as the initial experimental domains. Give each claim a baseline, partition policy, total budget, power or precision justification, statistic, multiplicity policy and PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE/INVALID conditions. A small pilot may remain inconclusive.

Require the full system to compete with its simplest competent predecessor at matched total cost. Keep mechanisms experimental or disable them if they do not add supported benefit.

Retain the lean stack and source layout unless a measured need justifies a change: Python 3.12, strict Pydantic records, SQLite with a single writer, immutable content-addressed artifacts, append-only exports, Linux isolation, pinned dependencies and pytest. Resolve exact tool versions from available sources; invent no hashes or reproducibility guarantees. Preserve existing resource defaults and domain-specific exceptions. Paid API spending stays zero until explicitly configured and authorized.
